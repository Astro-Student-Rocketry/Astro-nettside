Darkstar was ASTRO's very first rocket: a small, deliberately simple build made
to prove the concept. The goal was never record altitude or top speed — it was
to take the full chain, from design through build to launch, all the way
through, and learn from every step.

For a brand-new organisation, Darkstar was the proof that we could go from idea
to a rocket that actually left the ground. It is the foundation everything else
in the programme builds on. And for a few seconds, it was the highest point in
Bergen.

![Darkstar climbing away from Børslid, trailing a column of smoke](/images/launches/Darkstar/darkstar-liftoff.png)

## The rocket

![Darkstar fully built, standing on a table at HVL](/images/launches/Darkstar/darkstar-ferdig.jpeg)

Simple to design and simple to build — precisely because the point was getting
through the whole process, not chasing performance. The airframe was built
around 3D-printed parts and a tube, with a flight computer in the nose logging
altitude and acceleration.

![Darkstar components on the workbench during the build](/images/launches/Darkstar/darkstar-bygging.jpeg)

## The motor cluster

![The aft end of Darkstar with seven Klima D9-P motors and igniter leads](/images/launches/Darkstar/darkstar-motorklynge.jpeg)

Propulsion came from a cluster of seven Klima D9-P motors, lit simultaneously.
Enough thrust for a small rocket, while each individual motor stayed an
off-the-shelf item we could handle safely.

## Key numbers

- **Apogee:** 650 m measured (800 m theoretical)
- **Max speed:** 500 km/h
- **Motor:** 7 × Klima D9-P
- **Liftoff mass:** 800 g
- **Flight time:** 70 seconds

## After the flight


The rocket was recovered.
the flight computer had data, and the parts were in good enough shape to study.

## Status

Flown. Proof of concept — the groundwork for everything that came after.
