Altair was the step up from Darkstar: a larger rocket running on solid fuel, a
more ambitious project in every respect. The launch was planned from Utsira in
June 2026.

It had to be called off. The motor had to be sourced from abroad, and the import
never went through. It is a reminder that rocketry in Norway is as much about
regulation and logistics as it is about engineering — and a challenge we are
still working to solve.

![Size comparison: Altair next to an adult person](/images/launches/Altair/altair-size.png)

## The rocket

![CAD model of Altair seen from the side, with fins and nose cone](/images/launches/Altair/altair-cad.png)

Altair is substantially bigger than Darkstar — over two metres long and built
around a single solid motor rather than a cluster of small ones. The airframe is
split into sections with couplers, so payload, avionics and recovery can be
built and tested separately.

![Cutaway CAD model showing avionics, recovery and motor section inside Altair](/images/launches/Altair/altair-cutaway.png)

The internals house the flight computer, parachute deployment and room for a
payload — much of the same architecture we carry forward into later projects.

## The build

![Altair fully built, standing in a window at HVL](/images/launches/Altair/altair-ferdig.png)

Construction and preparation got a long way: the rocket was built, painted and
made ready. Without a motor, the launch still could not happen.

![An ASTRO member holding the finished Altair rocket](/images/launches/Altair/altair-medlem.jpeg)

The experience has shaped how we think about motor procurement going forward —
and is one of the reasons we are working towards developing our own motor.

## Status

Aborted. The rocket was built, but the motor never cleared import.
